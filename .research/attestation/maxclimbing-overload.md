# Max Climbing — Progressive Overload in a Variability Sport

## Summary

Argues that muscle/connective tissue adapts to repeated high mechanical tension that
exceeds prior exposure, requiring sufficient intensity, sufficient frequency, and
progression over time. Climbing's variability is good for skill but distributes
stress so widely that no single movement pattern gets consistent progressive
overload — maximal effort does not guarantee a strengthening stimulus, and the body
self-optimizes around its limiter. The article's proposed remedies are structural:
dedicated off-the-wall strength phases and "reduced variability blocks." (It does not
itself prescribe board climbing as the solution.)

## Key passages

- "Muscle and connective tissue adapt when exposed to repeated, high mechanical tension that exceeds prior exposure." (intensity + frequency + progression required)
- "No single movement pattern receives consistent progressive overload."
- "Effort may be maximal. Stimulus may not be."
- "What makes you complete the move may prevent you from strengthening the limiter."
- Proposed approaches include "Dedicated strength phases off the wall" and "Reduced variability blocks."
